TASK 2
Implementation and Evaluation of Near-Field Integrated Sensing, Computing and Semantic Communication for DT-Assisted Vehicular Networks
Reference paper: Y. Yang, Y. Ding, J. Wang, Z. Yang, C. Zhu, Z. Zhang, D. Niyato and M. Shikh-Bahaei, "Near-Field Integrated Sensing, Computing and Semantic Communication in Digital Twin-Assisted Vehicular Networks," IEEE Transactions on Vehicular Technology, 2026, DOI 10.1109/TVT.2026.3683229.

PROBLEM STATEMENT
The previous task assumes the digital twin is synchronised from data the vehicle itself senses and uploads. This task examines the alternative: the RSU senses the vehicles directly. Using a large millimetre-wave antenna array, the RSU both illuminates the road to estimate vehicle position and velocity, and serves those same vehicles with semantic communication over the identical spectrum - an integrated sensing, computing and semantic communication (ISCSC) design.
At mmWave frequencies with a large array, vehicles fall within the array's near-field region, where the conventional plane-wave assumption fails and the spherical wavefront must be modelled explicitly. This complicates beamforming, but it also enables joint angle and distance estimation from a single RSU, which a far-field model cannot provide.
The framework must therefore solve three coupled problems simultaneously: track vehicle state accurately enough to maintain the twin, allocate RSU computational resources across vehicles, and design beamforming and semantic extraction ratios that satisfy both sensing accuracy and communication rate requirements. This task reproduces that framework and evaluates its cost relative to the device-side approach in Task 1.

OBJECTIVES
Reproduce the near-field system model: RSUs equipped with a uniform linear array of Nt half-wavelength-spaced antennas in a MU-MIMO mmWave configuration, with near-field coverage of approximately 300 m. Model vehicle positions as a Poisson distribution over a highway segment of 100 m x 10 m, with vehicles of 4.5 m x 2 m and a minimum inter-vehicle distance of 3.5 m.
Implement the near-field steering vector and channel model, retaining the spherical-wavefront dependence on both angle and distance. Verify it against the far-field approximation and identify the range at which the two diverge materially.
Implement and compare the three vehicle tracking filters evaluated in the paper - a particle filter at Ns = 500 and Ns = 2000 particles, an extended Kalman filter (EKF), and an unscented Kalman filter (UKF). Measure angle, distance and velocity RMSE and average computation time per filter as the number of vehicles grows.
Implement the resource allocation and beamforming optimisation, including the simulated annealing procedure for vehicle-to-RSU assignment, subject to the sensing accuracy and semantic rate constraints. Evaluate the Cramer-Rao bound (CRB) for angle estimation as the sensing accuracy benchmark.
Reproduce the paper's headline claim - a 20% improvement in transmission rate over a conventional ISAC scheme at equal sensing accuracy - and evaluate it across the reported data sizes of 1 Kb, 10 Kb and 100 Kb. State clearly whether the claim reproduces.
Deliver a documented Python implementation, a reproduction report distinguishing reproduced from non-reproduced results, and all measurements required by the comparison framework below.
